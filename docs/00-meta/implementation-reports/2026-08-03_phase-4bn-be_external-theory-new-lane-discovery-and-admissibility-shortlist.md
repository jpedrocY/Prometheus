# Phase 4bn-BE — External-Theory New-Lane Discovery and Mechanism-Admissibility Shortlist

## 1. Phase identity

Phase 4bn-BE — External-Theory New-Lane Discovery and Mechanism-Admissibility Shortlist.

A **docs-only external-theory research and governance screening** phase. It asks whether external primary
theory supports any genuinely new Prometheus research mechanism family that is materially distinct from
every stopped, rejected, depleted, or retained family; that is scientifically meaningful without strategy
construction; that is preregisterable before market-data access; that rests on an admissible and
realistically obtainable data source; that carries a legitimate decision consequence on both pass and
fail; that is proportionate to its likely evidence and engineering cost; and that does not depend on
opening any evidence reserve.

It produces a neutral research dossier for a later independent Fable review. **It does not select or
authorize a new lane.**

**Risk tier.** Tier 1 / Full Phase per `docs/00-meta/process/phase-risk-tiering-standard.md`. The phase
may influence future scientific direction even though it opens no market data and implements nothing, so
it takes the highest ceremony tier despite mutating no eligibility, manifest, verdict, reserve, ledger,
or lock.

`Phase 4bn-BD` is the rejected CF-1 filter-continuation title recorded by Phase 4bn-BC. Nothing in this
phase reuses, redefines, or creates anything under that identifier.

## 5. External-research method

The search proceeded in one direction only, and the direction is the phase's causal-order requirement:

```text
external mechanism theory
→ observable mechanism implication
→ admissible data requirement
→ non-strategy research question
→ frozen falsification design
→ legitimate decision consequence
```

No candidate was generated from a Prometheus result, an available feature column, an existing
implementation capability, a desire to rescue a stopped arc, a model family, a threshold, a preferred
target, a convenient dataset, or a possible trading use.

**Procedure.**

1. Enumerate the mechanism families that external market-microstructure, asset-pricing, and
   derivatives-design theory currently treats as established or actively studied, without reference to
   what Prometheus happens to hold on disk.
2. For each family, state the observable the theory predicts.
3. Only then check the observable against the committed data-availability record (Phase 4at §6; Phase
   4bn-AW §11; Phase 4bn-AB as restated) and the committed prohibition set.
4. Reject on the first binding ground; record the ground.
5. For anything surviving, construct the full card required by §14 of the phase mandate, including the
   strongest technically credible case against it.

Search was broad enough to test whether a genuinely new source exists. No candidate was created to fill
a quota, and zero was treated throughout as an acceptable — and possibly preferable — result.

**External actions performed.** Bibliographic searches; retrieval of publisher, repository, author, and
official-documentation web pages; DOI and citation verification. Four ordinary research PDFs were fetched
into a temporary non-repository tool-results directory by the retrieval tool; none was committed, none is
under `D:\Prometheus`, and page-level rendering of them was unavailable in this environment (recorded in
§6 as a retrieval limitation, not resolved by any other means).

**External actions not performed.** No Binance API or exchange endpoint call; no market-data download of
any kind; no historical trade, quote, order-book, liquidation, funding, open-interest, or derivatives
data acquisition; no authenticated service; no credential or `.env` use; no WebSocket; no MCP or
Graphify; no package installation; no external repository clone; no execution of external code; no
executable download. No external datum was used to calculate any Prometheus result.

## 6. Source-quality standard

The hierarchy applied is the one Phase 4bn-AT established, extended to academic sources:

- **Primary — admitted for substantive claims.** Peer-reviewed journal articles; working papers from
  recognized academic or institutional repositories (arXiv, NBER, BIS, SSRN author-of-record postings);
  official exchange market-structure and contract documentation; official dataset documentation; authors'
  own technical appendices, replication packages, and project sites.
- **Secondary — location aid only.** Search-result summaries, bibliographic aggregators, package
  registries, blogs, newsletters, vendor material, social media. These may locate a primary source and
  **carry no substantive claim in this report**.

Publication venue, citation count, and author reputation are **not** treated as evidence that a mechanism
is admissible for Prometheus. Repository documents outrank external theory absolutely for the status of
Prometheus decisions, locks, prior work, and authorization.

## 8. Search-space definition

### 8.1 What was searched

External primary theory across four bodies of literature, selected because each proposes mechanisms with
*observable implications* rather than trading rules:

1. **Market-microstructure measurement theory** — transaction-cost and liquidity estimation from limited
   price data; noise and sampling theory; order-flow statistical structure; price impact.
2. **Asset-pricing and derivatives-design theory specific to perpetual futures** — the funding mechanism,

### NL-C1 — Price-only effective-spread estimation for the admissible substrate

#### A. Candidate identity

- **Identifier:** `NL-C1`
- **Mechanism-family name:** Price-only (quote-free) effective bid-ask spread estimation from transaction
  and range data.
- **One-sentence non-trading research question:** *Can the effective bid-ask spread of the BTCUSDT
  USDⓈ-M perpetual over an admissible non-reserve window be estimated from transaction and OHLC prices
  alone, to a standard that externally validated estimator theory would accept, given that no quoted-spread
  ground truth is admissible for that window?*

#### B. External mechanism source

**Causal mechanism in plain language.** When a two-sided quote exists, transactions print alternately near
the bid and near the ask even when the underlying efficient price does not move. This mechanical
alternation leaves a signature in observed prices — negative serial covariance in successive transaction
price changes (S1), and a systematic inflation of the observed high-low range relative to the range the
efficient price alone would generate (S2, S3, S4). The size of that signature is a monotone function of
the spread. The spread is therefore *recoverable from prices alone*, without ever observing a quote,
provided the model's assumptions hold.

**Primary sources and what each establishes.**

- **S1 (Roll 1984)** establishes the covariance identity `S = 2·sqrt(|Cov(ΔP_t, ΔP_{t-1})|)` under a
  random-walk efficient price, no information effects, a constant spread, and order-flow independence.
  *What it does not establish:* it does not measure total trading cost. Roll states the estimator captures
  the effective spread component only, excluding information-asymmetry and inventory costs, and he
  explicitly records that the estimator **fails** when the estimated serial covariance is positive.
- **S2 (Corwin & Schultz 2012)** and **S3 (Abdi & Ranaldo 2017)** establish that daily range and
  close-high-low information identify the spread by separating a variance component that scales with the
  observation interval from a spread component that does not. *What they do not establish:* validity
  outside the equity samples on which they were developed and tested.
- **S4 (Ardia, Guidotti & Kroencke 2024)** establishes that the earlier estimators are downward biased
  under infrequent trading and use only a subset of the OHLC information, and that an optimal combination
  ("EDGE") is asymptotically unbiased and minimum-variance, delivering the most accurate estimates
  available **in the absence of quote data**. *What it does not establish, as verified in this phase:* a
  quoted-spread ground-truth validation for crypto. Its published open datasets include monthly spread
  **estimates** for Binance crypto pairs, but the author project site does not state a crypto benchmark,
  and the article's full assumption set could not be retrieved here (§6.3).
- **S5 (Goyenko, Holden & Trzcinka 2009)** establishes the *methodology by which such estimators are
  legitimately validated* — horseraces against TAQ effective-spread, realized-spread, and price-impact
  benchmarks. *What it does not establish:* anything about crypto perpetuals; its ground truth is US
  equities, 1993–2005.

**Applicability to perpetual futures.** **By inference, and the inference is explicitly labelled.** None
of S1–S3, S5 concerns crypto perpetuals. S4 applies its estimator to Binance crypto pairs and thus treats
the setting as in-scope, but this phase could not verify that the application was validated against a
quoted-spread benchmark. The mechanism itself — discrete transaction prices bouncing within a spread — is
a property of any quote-driven order book, so the *theory* transfers cleanly; the *calibration and bias
properties* transfer only by inference.

#### C. Observable implication

**Exact observable.** For a chosen bar frequency over an admissible window, the estimators produce a
sequence of effective-spread estimates in relative (basis-point) units. The theory predicts three
structural things simultaneously, and it is the conjunction that is testable:

1. the Roll covariance is **negative** (the bounce signature exists at all);
2. the range-based estimators (S2, S3, S4) return **positive, finite, mutually coherent** estimates on the
   same window;
3. the estimates behave as theory requires under sampling-frequency variation — the bias direction under
   infrequent trading that S4 characterizes should be visible in the disagreement pattern between the
   older estimators and the efficient one.

**Why the observable identifies the mechanism rather than merely correlating with it.** The estimators are
not fitted; each is a closed-form transformation derived from an explicit structural model of the bounce.
Agreement between structurally distinct derivations — a covariance identity (S1) and two independent
range identities (S2, S3) optimally combined (S4) — is a genuine over-identification test: three routes to
the same latent parameter that need not agree unless the common structural assumption holds.

**Major competing explanations.** (i) Genuine short-horizon negative return autocorrelation from
inventory or overreaction effects, which would masquerade as spread in S1; (ii) time-varying spread within
the estimation window, violating the constant-spread assumption and biasing every estimator; (iii)
intraday volatility periodicity contaminating the range decomposition; (iv) an effectively binding tick
size, which would compress the spread onto a discrete grid and degenerate the estimators (see N-11).

**Identifiable with Prometheus-accessible data?** The *estimates* are computable from admissible data. The
*validity* of the estimates on this venue and window is **not** locally checkable, because the only object
that could check them is a quoted spread, and `HIST_TOB_BOOKTICKER_SOURCE` is
`INADMISSIBLE_OR_UNAVAILABLE`. This is the candidate's central and unresolved weakness, and it is stated
again in §L.

  spread agree with one another on the same admissible window to within the tolerance their own theory
  predicts.*

  efficient combination whose bias direction S4 characterizes. No arbitrary comparator is invented.
- **Target / outcome:** the effective-spread estimate in relative units.
- **Unit of observation:** one bar-frequency-by-window estimation cell.
- **Horizon:** none — this is a level, not a forecast. This is a deliberate property: the candidate cannot
  become a prediction problem.
- **Split principle:** none required for a non-predictive estimate; the honest analogue is **block
  consistency** across the same UTC date/month block structure the project already uses, so that a result
  cannot rest on one period.
- **Primary test statistic:** a disagreement measure across estimators, aggregated at block level.
- **Pre-data invalidation conditions:** the tick-size question above unresolved; the estimators' own
  documented minimum-observation and minimum-trade-frequency requirements unmet at the chosen frequency;
  or an inability to state the tolerance from theory rather than from the data.
- **Pass condition:** block-consistent agreement within a tolerance fixed before any computation.
- **Fail condition:** the estimators disagree beyond that tolerance, or Roll's covariance is positive on a
  material share of blocks — Roll's own stated failure mode, which would indicate the structural model
  does not describe this instrument.
- **Invalid-run condition:** any coverage, integrity, or degeneracy screen breached, on the fail-closed
  pattern Phase 4bn-AT §36 established.

**No threshold is invented here.** The tolerance is deliberately left unstated because neither external
theory nor binding governance forces a value, and inventing one in this phase would be precisely the
researcher-freedom hazard §I identifies.

### NL-C2 — Perpetual-futures funding mechanism as a no-arbitrage friction bound

#### A. Candidate identity

- **Identifier:** `NL-C2`
- **Mechanism-family name:** Perpetual-futures contract design and limits to arbitrage — the funding
  mechanism as an observable no-arbitrage anchoring relation.
- **One-sentence non-trading research question:** *Does the archived Binance funding-rate series for the
  BTCUSDT perpetual, read through the exchange's own published funding formula and the no-arbitrage
  bounds of perpetual-futures pricing theory, carry identifying information about the magnitude of the
  frictions that anchor the perpetual to its index — or is it uninformative because the published rate is
  interval-censored by the clamp?*

#### B. External mechanism source

**Causal mechanism in plain language.** A perpetual futures contract has no expiry, so nothing forces
convergence to spot. Instead, the exchange makes longs pay shorts (or the reverse) a periodic funding
payment proportional to the perpetual's premium over an index of spot prices. That payment is the
anchoring force. In a frictionless market the perpetual price is pinned to a deterministic multiple of
spot; with trading costs, it is pinned only to within a band whose width is the round-trip cost of the
arbitrage. Observed deviations therefore carry information about the frictions that keep arbitrageurs from
closing them.

**Primary sources and what each establishes.**

- **S6 (He, Manela, Ross & von Wachter 2024)** derives, in Proposition 1, the frictionless no-arbitrage
  price `F_t = [κ/(κ − (r − r'))]·S_t` where `κ` is funding-rate intensity, `r` is the cash borrowing rate
  and `r'` the asset holding rate; and in Proposition 2, with round-trip trading cost `C`, the bound
  `|F_t − [κ/(κ − (r − r'))]·S_t| ≤ C`. It establishes empirically that deviations in crypto are larger
  than in traditional currency markets, comove across currencies, and diminish over time, and that the
  deviation magnitude is comparable to the theoretical bound calibrated to actual trading fees. *What it
  does not establish:* it abstracts from margin and liquidation risk, assumes a constant interest rate,
  approximates 8-hourly clamped funding by continuous payments, and assumes bounded deviations. Its sample
  is 2020–2022. **It also reports that an implied arbitrage strategy yields high Sharpe ratios — a strategy
  claim this candidate does not adopt, does not test, and is barred from pursuing.**
- **S7 (Ackerer, Hugonnier & Jermann 2024/2026)** independently derives no-arbitrage prices for linear,
  inverse, and quanto perpetuals in discrete and continuous time, shows the futures price equals the
  risk-neutral expectation of spot sampled at a random time reflecting anchoring intensity, and identifies
  funding specifications under which futures and spot prices coincide and the contract is replicable.
  *What it does not establish:* any friction magnitude on any specific venue. It is a pricing-theory
  result under frictionless replication assumptions.
- **S8 (Schmeling, Schrimpf & Todorov, BIS WP 1087)** establishes that the crypto futures-spot gap is
  large and time-varying — averaging above 10% p.a. and occasionally exceeding 40% p.a. — and attributes
  the variation **mainly to convenience yields from trend-chasing demand and to limited arbitrage
  capital**. *What it does not establish:* that the gap measures transaction cost. It says close to the
  opposite, and this is the candidate's decisive confound.
- **S9 (Binance official funding documentation)** establishes the exact published relation. Two features
  are load-bearing. First, the premium index is built from **impact bid and ask fill prices** — a
  depth-derived object — averaged over the funding interval. Second, the funding rate applies a clamp:
  `F = P + clamp(i − P, +0.05%, −0.05%)`, so whenever the premium index sits within 5 basis points of the
  interest-rate term the published rate is pinned at the interest term and reveals only an interval, while
  outside that band the published rate moves one-for-one with the premium index. *What it does not
  establish:* the 2024-window values of the maintenance-margin-ratio cap, the tick size, or the Impact
  Margin Notional for BTCUSDT.

**Applicability to perpetual futures.** **Directly and specifically.** Unlike every other family considered
in this phase, this mechanism is *about* perpetual futures and about the exact venue and contract the
project studies. No cross-asset inference is required for the mechanism itself. Inference is required only
for the empirical magnitudes, since S6's sample predates the Prometheus window and S8's sample is broader
than one venue.

#### C. Observable implication

**Exact observable.** The archived funding-rate series for BTCUSDT. The theory plus S9's published formula
predict a specific, checkable structure:

1. the published rate should be **pinned at the interest-rate term** in the interval where the premium
   index lies within the clamp dead zone, and should track the premium index one-for-one outside it — so
   the empirical distribution of the funding rate should show a **point mass at the interest term** and a
   continuous body outside it;
2. the width of the region over which the perpetual's premium wanders without provoking arbitrage should
   be **bounded**, per S6 Proposition 2, by round-trip frictions;
3. the deviation series should exhibit the persistence and time-variation S6 and S8 both report, rather
   than white noise.

The censoring is not a defect to be worked around; it is a *prediction*. If the point mass is absent, the
published formula does not describe the series and the entire reading collapses — a clean, theory-derived
kill.

**Why this identifies the mechanism rather than merely correlating with it.** The relation between the
published funding rate and the premium index is not statistical; it is a **contractual identity published
by the exchange**. Given the formula, the funding series is an exact, piecewise-invertible,
interval-censored transform of the premium index. There is no fitting, no proxy, and no estimation of the
link function.

**Major competing explanations.** These are severe and are stated at full strength. Per **S8**, basis
deviations are driven mainly by **convenience yields and limited arbitrage capital**, not by transaction
cost. Per **S6's own caveats**, margin requirements, liquidation risk, counterparty and exchange risk, and
funding-of-collateral costs all sit inside the arbitrageur's effective `C`. The bound therefore measures
*total limits to arbitrage for the marginal participant*, of which execution cost is one unseparated
component. Any claim that a recovered band measures the project's execution cost would be false.
Additionally, the clamp cap and the interest-rate default are exchange policy parameters that can change,
and their 2024 values are not established here.

**Identifiable with Prometheus-accessible data?** Partly and asymmetrically. The censored-recovery form
uses funding-rate history that the committed record places on disk, and needs no acquisition. The
uncensored form — reading the premium directly — requires premium-index or index-price klines, which are
archive-available but **not acquired**.

#### F. Identification and falsification (feasibility sketch, not a preregistration)

- **Target / outcome:** the recovered premium interval per settlement, and the empirical bound on its

- **Unit of observation:** one 8-hour funding settlement.
- **Horizon:** none — this is a contemporaneous identity, not a forecast. As with NL-C1, this is a

- **Split principle:** none required; block consistency across the project's existing UTC date/month block

- **Pre-data invalidation conditions:** the 2024 cap, interest-rate, and IMN parameters not establishable

- **Fail condition:** the series is dominated by the dead zone or by the cap to the point that no

### 19.3 Surviving candidates in compressed neutral form

**NL-C1 — Price-only effective-spread estimation.** *Theory:* transaction prices bounce within the spread,
leaving a negative serial-covariance signature (Roll 1984) and an inflated high-low range (Corwin &
Schultz 2012; Abdi & Ranaldo 2017), from which the effective spread is recoverable in closed form; Ardia,
Guidotti & Kroencke (2024, JFE) combine these optimally into an estimator that is the most accurate
available *in the absence of quote data*. *Observable:* mutually coherent, block-consistent spread
estimates from three structurally distinct derivations on the same window. *Data:* already on disk;
non-reserve; no acquisition; no reserve. *Question:* can the effective spread of this instrument be
estimated to a standard the estimator theory would accept, given that **no quoted-spread ground truth is
admissible for the window**? *Pass:* narrows, prospectively, the project's standing claim that cost
realism is structurally unreachable from admissible data. *Fail:* closes that question permanently.
*Central weakness:* validity cannot be checked locally; the standard validation apparatus (Goyenko, Holden
& Trzcinka 2009) requires quote data the project cannot admit. *Nearest trap:* the stopped bounce arc,
whose mechanism is the same bounce.

**NL-C2 — Perpetual funding mechanism as a no-arbitrage friction bound.** *Theory:* a perpetual has no
expiry, so periodic funding payments are the only force anchoring it to a spot index; He, Manela, Ross &
von Wachter (2024) derive the frictionless no-arbitrage price and, with round-trip trading cost `C`, the
bound `|F − no-arbitrage price| ≤ C`; Ackerer, Hugonnier & Jermann (2024/2026) derive the pricing relation
independently. The exchange's own published formula makes the funding rate an exact, piecewise-invertible,
**interval-censored** transform of a depth-derived premium index (censored whenever the premium sits within
5 bps of an interest term). *Observable:* the archived funding series should show a point mass at the
interest term and a bounded, persistent body outside it. *Data:* funding history already on disk;
non-reserve; no acquisition for the primary form; no reserve. *Question:* does the archived funding series
carry identifying information about the anchoring band, or is it censored into silence? *Pass:* a
theory-anchored, venue-specific interval on total limits to arbitrage. *Fail:* closes the last unexploited
admissible data family. *Central weakness:* BIS WP 1087 (Schmeling, Schrimpf & Todorov) attributes crypto
basis variation **mainly to convenience yields and limited arbitrage capital**, so the band conflates
execution cost with capital scarcity and cannot be decomposed. *Nearest trap:* a retained verdict in which
funding was used as a directional contrarian trigger; the governing lane is flagged as high rescue risk.
